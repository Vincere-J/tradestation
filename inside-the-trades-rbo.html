How RBO trades

Looks forAn opening range breakout on the Micro Russell (M2K). RBO marks the high and low of price from 9:10 AM to 10:00 AM ET (9:20 AM to 10:00 AM in these backtests), then trades a break of that range, long or short.
When it trades9:35 AM to 11:00 AM ET, Monday to Friday. Range 9:10 AM to 10:00 AM ET (9:20 AM to 10:00 AM in these backtests). No new shorts after 10:25 AM. At most 2 trades a day.
Stop and targetsStop 10 points from entry, 3 targets, 15, 17.5 and 25 points away. After 10 points of profit the stop moves to the entry price plus 2.5 points, so the trade is protected. A trailing stop starts after 15 points of profit and follows 10 points behind. Any trade still open closes at 4:30 PM EST.
Contracts and why6 contracts, 2 for each of the 3 targets. Planned risk about $300. These backtests ran with Edge Leverage on, so the next trade after a stop out that day used 12 contracts. Edge Leverage is now off on RBO.
In the backtestBacktest period 10/1/2026 to 10/23/2026: 14 trades, 6 winners and 8 losers, net -$988.80 after fees.
Patterns in this backtest14 trades on 12 trading days, up to 2 trades a day (2 days had more than 1). 3 long and 11 short. Entries from 10:10 AM to 11:02 AM ET. Of the 14 trades: 4 reached PT1; 3 reached PT2; 2 reached PT3; 3 had contracts closed by a stop already moved into profit; 7 were full stop losses; 2 were closed by the 4:30 PM ET end of day exit. Average winner +$317.20, average loser -$361.50. Time in a trade: median 44 minutes, shortest 6 minutes, longest about 6 hours 19 minutes.

Quick terms

ShowHide
BacktestA test of an algo's rules on past market data. These examples come from tick data backtests in the NinjaTrader Strategy Analyzer, not live trading, and results include trading fees.
Long or shortLong means buying to profit if price rises. Short means selling first to profit if price falls.
ContractOne unit of a futures market. RBO trades the Micro Russell (M2K).
Point and tickA point (pts) is one full unit of price movement. A tick is the smallest step price can move.
SetupThe market pattern an algo waits for before it is allowed to enter.
EntryThe moment and price where the algo opens the trade.
StopThe price where a losing trade is closed automatically. It is set before the trade is entered, so the loss is planned to be about a set size. Fees, and fills a little past the stop price, can make it a little larger.
Target (PT)A price where profit is taken. With more than one contract, part of the position exits at each target (PT1, PT2, PT3).
DEC26The December 2026 futures contract, the one these backtests traded.
Trailing stopA stop that follows a winning trade, so it locks in profit if price turns back.
Breakeven plusOnce a trade is far enough in profit, the stop moves to the entry price plus a small amount, so the trade is protected.
Edge LeverageA setting that makes the next trade after a stop out that day use more contracts. It was on for RBO in these backtests, so the next trade after a stop out that day used 12 contracts instead of 6. Edge Leverage is now off on RBO.
CandleOne bar on the chart that covers a set amount of time, like 5 minutes. The thick body shows where price opened and closed in that time, and the thin lines above and below show the highest and lowest prices it reached.
Candle close vs touchRBO enters the moment price moves through its range level. It does not wait for a candle to close.
Reading the chartsGreen candles closed higher than they opened, red candles closed lower. The blue arrow is the entry, a black X is an exit, the red dashed line is the stop and the green dashed lines are targets. An amber shaded box marks the setup on most charts. Times are Eastern, and each candle is labeled by the time it closed, the same as NinjaTrader.
