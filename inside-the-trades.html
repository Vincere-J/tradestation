The big picture

Different styles, different days. A breakout, a squeeze and a trend style do not all need the same market. Because each style looks for a different kind of market, the algos often trade on different days and do not all win or lose at the same time.

Stops and targets come first. For every algo here, the stop and the targets are set before the trade is entered. The planned risk to the stop in these examples:

Losses can be larger than planned. In the backtests some losses were larger than planned, for example OGX -$463.20 on a 4 contract trade (10/8/2026) and B2X -$414.40 (9/14/2026).

One loss does not end the day. On OGX Example B and B2X Examples C and B, a stop out was followed later that day by a winning trade. Other days, like MST2 on 9/24/2026, finished with a net loss in the backtest even though they included a winner.

Why a losing trade is shown on purpose

Losing trades are a normal part of every trading style. Each algo sets its stop and targets before the trade is even entered, so a loss is planned to be about a set size. Seeing a loser next to two winners shows how the plan works when a setup does not follow through. Losses can also be a little larger than planned after fees, or when the stop fills past its price.

How to read a trade

The numbers on the drawing match the five steps below.

A simple drawing of a long trade, for illustration only. If price had fallen to the red line instead, the stop would have closed the trade.

  1. 1SetupThe pattern the algo waits for, like an opening range, a squeeze or a momentum turn.
  2. 2EntryWhere the trade opens. Some algos enter on a touch, others on a candle close.
  3. 3StopSet before entry. If price moves the wrong way this far, the trade closes.
  4. 4TargetsSet before entry. Part of the position can exit at each target.
  5. 5ExitThe trade ends at a target, the stop or a trailing stop.

Quick terms

ShowHide
BacktestA test of an algo's rules on past market data. These examples come from tick data backtests in the NinjaTrader Strategy Analyzer, not live trading, and results include trading fees.
Long or shortLong means buying to profit if price rises. Short means selling first to profit if price falls.
ContractOne unit of a futures market. These algos trade the Micro Nasdaq (MNQ), the Micro Russell (M2K), the Micro S&P 500 (MES) and the full size Dow (YM).
Point and tickA point (pts) is one full unit of price movement. A tick is the smallest step price can move.
SetupThe market pattern an algo waits for before it is allowed to enter.
EntryThe moment and price where the algo opens the trade.
StopThe price where a losing trade is closed automatically. It is set before the trade is entered, so the loss is planned to be about a set size. Fees, and fills a little past the stop price, can make it a little larger.
Target (PT)A price where profit is taken. With more than one contract, part of the position exits at each target (PT1, PT2, PT3).
DEC26The December 2026 futures contract, the one these backtests traded.
Trailing stopA stop that follows a winning trade, so it locks in profit if price turns back.
Edge LeverageA setting that makes the next trade after a stop out that day use more contracts. It was on for OGX and RBO in these backtests: the next OGX trade after a stop out that day used 4 contracts instead of 2, and the next RBO trade used 12 instead of 6. Edge Leverage is now on only for OGX.
CandleOne bar on the chart that covers a set amount of time, like 5 minutes. The thick body shows where price opened and closed in that time, and the thin lines above and below show the highest and lowest prices it reached.
Candle close vs touchOGX and RBO enter the moment price moves through their range level. URGO, B2X, MST2 and G4M wait for a candle to close before they enter.
Reading the chartsGreen candles closed higher than they opened, red candles closed lower. The blue arrow is the entry, a black X is an exit, the red dashed line is the stop and the green dashed lines are targets. An amber shaded box marks the setup on most charts.
